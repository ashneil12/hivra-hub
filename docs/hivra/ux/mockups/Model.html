How Hivra fits together

Today each feature added its own corner of the screen and nobody owned the whole. This is the one model every screen follows from now on: three places, one list of what needs you, and one name for each thing.

Three places

Home

Everything across your projects. Ask your Chief of Staff anything or hand work to any project, then see what needs you, what's working and what got done today.

A project

The coding team chat is the project's home. Its tasks, pages, chats and Monos sit beside it in the sidebar, and the Crew panel on the right shows every worker, its pull request and its checks.

Settings

Only settings, in about twelve groups instead of 28 pages. Things you look at, like activity, usage and past decisions, move to where you'd look for them.

One list: Needs you

Stopped workers, questions, approvals and work ready to merge all go into one list. The rail badges, the sidebar count, Home, a project's header and a Mono's red line all count that same list, so they always agree. Nothing says "All quiet" while it has anything in it. Today twelve different pieces of code each decide this on their own, and that's why the screen contradicts itself.

What goes away

  • Autopilot and Hub icons in the rail. Autopilot becomes a switch in each project's header, and its log moves to Settings.
  • The projects list on Home. The rail already is that list, now with a red count and a working dot on each project.
  • Home's pretend chat, which filed your questions as tasks. Your real Chief of Staff answers instead and hands work to the right project.
  • Loose "Move up", "Hide" and "Pin a panel" text. One Customize button holds it.
  • Badges counted from placeholder data, Ash OS leftovers, the update row that can't update, and the upstream GitHub star prompt.
  • The Automations gallery of someone else's templates. It starts from your projects and Monos instead.
  • Emoji reactions that look like buttons, and usage lines repeated in three places. Usage lives once, in the status bar.

How it gets built

  1. One Needs-you list that every count reads. First, because everything else depends on it.
  2. Home: the real Chief of Staff chat, then Needs you, Working now and Done today.
  3. Rail and sidebar: places only, a project sidebar, and one Coding team entry.
  4. The project screen: a header with its counts and the Autopilot switch, and the Crew panel on the right.
  5. Words and leftovers: the names above, Settings regrouped, and dead parts removed.

Each step comes back with before and after screenshots of a test copy, and I check them by eye against these screens before it merges.
